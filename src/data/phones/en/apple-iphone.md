On 9 January 2007, Apple introduced the iPhone as a phone, a touch-controlled iPod and an internet device. Finger-driven scrolling, photo zooming and browsing without a stylus changed the screen's role: it became the main interface rather than a companion to physical keys.

- **The web in your pocket.** Safari, email and maps brought familiar internet tasks to the phone. The original model used EDGE and Wi-Fi, without 3G.
- **An early version, not every later iPhone.** There was no App Store at launch, and the camera was simpler than those of many dedicated camera phones. Later capabilities should not be projected back onto this model.

US sales began on 29 June 2007. The 4 GB version cost $499 and the 8 GB version $599, with a carrier contract as an important purchase condition. In September, Apple reported one million phones sold in 74 days — a concrete milestone beyond the stage demonstration.
