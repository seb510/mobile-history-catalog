Nokia announced the N95 on 26 September 2006, with volume shipments planned for the first quarter of 2007. The catalogue uses its market-release year.

- **The idea.** Nokia's description of a “multimedia computer” captured its ambition: a camera, music player, organiser, internet access and navigation in one pocket-sized device.
- **Two sliding directions.** Sliding one way revealed the numeric keypad; sliding the other exposed media controls. The N95 remained a button-operated phone, but physically adapted to the task instead of relying entirely on menus.
- **Multimedia.** A 5-megapixel camera, GPS and fast mobile networks formed a shared scenario: capture an event, find a place and use internet services without carrying a separate device for each task.

A GPS receiver, mapping software and the availability of a navigation service are different things. Services should not be assumed identical across markets and years.

Nokia's initial estimated price was €550 before operator subsidies. The N95 represents a mature multifunctional phone before touch interfaces became dominant. Its significance lies in combining capabilities, rather than introducing GPS or Wi-Fi to mobile phones for the first time.

## N95 8GB version

Nokia announced the start of N95 8GB shipments on 15 October 2007. Its black body, larger display and higher-capacity battery developed the same dual-slider concept. A photograph of this version appears in the gallery above.

These are the specifications of the international 2007 version; Nokia introduced a separate US version in 2008. The additional “up to 100 MB” for applications and personal data quoted by Nokia must not be confused with RAM.
