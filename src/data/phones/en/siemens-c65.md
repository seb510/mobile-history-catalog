The C65 represents the more affordable side of Siemens' colour-phone line in 2004. A built-in camera, MMS and downloadable applications brought features into the lower tier that had recently been associated with more expensive phones. Its square display and conventional numeric keypad kept the form compact and familiar.

Photos could be used as wallpapers or contact images, or sent to someone else. The manual describes image-quality settings and an external flash accessory. That distinction matters: support for a flash did not mean every C65 had one built in.

CLIPit covers, themes and ringtones offered ways to personalise the device, while Games and Applications allowed users to add software. Moving from a monochrome Siemens to the C65 meant more than gaining colour; it opened up new forms of personal content, including photos, graphics and multimedia messages.

Alongside the CX65, the C65 shows two priorities from the same era: a more compact entry model and a phone with a larger rectangular screen. Both retained keypad controls while adding personal photos, colour styling and small applications to everyday messaging.
