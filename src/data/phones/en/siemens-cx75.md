Siemens showed the CX75 at CeBIT on 9 March 2005 alongside several other new models, and it went on sale that June in two colours — Titan Grey and Sand Silver. In the lineup it was a direct successor to the 2004 CX65: the same 132 × 176 px screen size, but noticeably updated internals.

The camera grew from the CX65's 0.3-megapixel VGA sensor to 1.3 MP with 5× digital zoom and video recording; digital zoom here shouldn't be confused with optical — frame resolution drops as you zoom in. The CX75 was also the first in the line to gain an RS-MMC expansion slot and Bluetooth 1.2 — neither of which the CX65 had, which was limited to an infrared port.

The phone leaned toward entertainment: preloaded games, swappable menu "skins," a photo editor, and an MP3 player, backed by 14 MB of built-in memory (plus a memory card for music and photos). That combination set the CX75 apart from the more business-oriented positioning of some rivals that year — the emphasis was on multimedia and personalisation rather than corporate features.
