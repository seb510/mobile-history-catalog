The Motorola Droid marked Android's transition from an early experiment to a platform for everyday tasks. A sliding QWERTY keyboard provided familiar text input, while its large-for-2009 display supported the web and maps without returning constantly to button-driven menus.

- **Navigation as a service.** Google Maps Navigation for Android 2.0 introduced spoken directions, automatic rerouting and online place search in October 2009. Verizon's Droid was to be its first phone, with the initial service launch limited to the US. The smartphone began taking over the role of a separate car navigator.
- **Droid is not identical to Milestone.** Verizon's US Droid used CDMA without a SIM card; the European Milestone used GSM/UMTS. The photograph here shows a Milestone. Listing both networks does not mean one handset supports both.

Beside the HTC Dream, it shows Android shifting from a new platform towards services that replaced other devices. The keyboard remained important to the body, while software increasingly defined its value.
