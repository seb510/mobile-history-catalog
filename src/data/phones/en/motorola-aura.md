With the AURA closed, its round display resembled a watch dial. To make a call you rotated the metal face sideways: the screen stayed in view and rows of number keys emerged below it. A window on the back offered a glimpse of the gears.

Motorola introduced the AURA in October 2008 at around $2,000 — one of the most expensive phones of its time. The display itself was circular, right down to the active image area. Its 62-carat sapphire cover was another detail borrowed from the world of mechanical watches. The body was machined from stainless steel with a chemically etched texture.

The AURA ran on 2G networks only (GSM, no 3G) and had no memory card slot — its 2 GB of built-in storage had to be shared between music, photos and apps. That's a deliberate trade-off for a premium accessory, not a technology flagship.
