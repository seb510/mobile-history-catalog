The S65 was Siemens' attempt to combine a business phone with more capable mobile photography. In the company's 2004 data sheet, it is presented as a work companion: contacts, email and an organizer sit alongside a megapixel camera and removable RS-MMC storage. The document scheduled its market launch for the third quarter of 2004.

The camera took stills at up to 1280 × 960, while short video clips used QCIF at 176 × 144. These modes should not be conflated: the megapixel resolution applied to photographs. The included 32 MB card helped store pictures, while the flash was a separate accessory rather than part of the built-in camera.

Its business features were equally specific: a 1,000-entry address book, calendar, tasks, notes and an email client. SyncML and Outlook synchronization could link the phone to work data; Bluetooth, infrared and a cable offered several ways to exchange it. This was not yet a modern smartphone with continuous cloud synchronization, but another way to carry part of the office.

Compared with the Nokia 6230, the S65 reflects a broader direction of the period: cameras and work tools were converging in conventional keypad phones. Its significance lies in that convergence, not only as a prelude to the later change in ownership of Siemens' mobile business.
