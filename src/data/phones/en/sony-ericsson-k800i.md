The 2006 K800i brought Sony's Cyber-shot camera identity to a mobile phone. Its classic body had two distinct sides: a screen and keypad for communication, and a covered camera for photography. A landscape menu and side shutter key reinforced the feel of a compact camera.

- **Light and timing.** The camera combined 3.2 megapixels, autofocus and a genuine xenon flash. Unlike the K750i's LED light, it changed how a scene was illuminated as well as the size of the photograph. BestPic helped select a moment from a sequence; editing tools worked on the phone itself.
- **Beyond capture.** Mobile photo blogging and PictBridge printing on compatible printers extended the sequence: see, shoot, select, share. This preceded social networks becoming the main destination for mobile photographs.

UMTS added video telephony and faster data transfer on compatible networks, while a music player and FM radio remained available. The K800i represents a mature button-operated camera phone: photography shaped its identity and construction, rather than being just one specification.
