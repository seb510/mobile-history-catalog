On 7 September 2005, Apple, Motorola and Cingular introduced the ROKR as the first phone with iTunes. It brought the familiar iPod library to a device already carried for calls. A dedicated music key switched between phone and player.

- **A library from your computer.** Music arrived from a Mac or PC over USB. Playlists could be copied manually or the phone automatically filled with music, audiobooks and podcasts. Apple's announcement specified a limit of 100 songs, rather than an unlimited music store inside the handset.
- **Music alongside calls.** The screen displayed album artwork, while stereo speakers and the included headset offered two ways to listen. Incoming calls paused playback, allowing music and communication to share one device.

The announced US price of $249.99 required a two-year Cingular commitment. The collaboration matters to this history; it does not establish that this phone's commercial fortunes alone caused the creation of the iPhone. Compared with the W800i, it shows two approaches from the same year: iTunes synchronisation and the Walkman identity on a mobile phone.

Source: [Apple, Motorola and Cingular — ROKR announcement, 7 September 2005](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2005/09/07Apple-Motorola-Cingular-Launch-Worlds-First-Mobile-Phone-with-iTunes/).
