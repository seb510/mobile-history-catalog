Nokia officially unveiled the N86 8MP on 17 February 2009 at Mobile World Congress in Barcelona — after the device had already leaked publicly in Singapore, with photos spreading online days before the formal announcement. The phone continued the N-series camera line after the N95 and N82, now with an 8-megapixel sensor and a wide 28 mm Carl Zeiss lens, an unusual combination for a mobile camera at the time.

The body used a dual slider mechanism: sliding the screen up revealed a numeric keypad for calls and messages, while sliding it down exposed separate multimedia keys. A third motion belonged to the back panel: a sliding module uncovered the camera lens and a new third-generation dual LED flash, physically separating the phone and photography use cases.

A variable aperture (f/2.4–f/4.8) and a mechanical shutter brought the N86's camera closer to a compact camera than a typical smartphone of the time. A 2.6-inch AMOLED screen, 8 GB of built-in storage and microSDHC support up to 16 GB rounded it out as a multimedia device, while an FM transmitter let it broadcast audio to an ordinary radio receiver.

Nokia's starting estimate was about €375 before taxes and subsidies, with retail availability planned for the second quarter of 2009. This was not a single dollar or regional price — configuration and cost varied by market.
