The 2003 Nokia 6600 combined a rounded body with an expandable Series 60 smartphone platform: a camera, media functions, memory-card support and third-party applications.

- **Software beyond the factory menu.** Installing applications made the phone adaptable to more tasks than a fixed set of built-in features.
- **A broad audience.** On 23 February 2004, Nokia reported shipments exceeding two million since the October 2003 introduction. This was a shipment milestone at that date, not lifetime retail sales.

Its openness also belongs to the history of mobile security. F-Secure describes Cabir as a Bluetooth worm targeting Symbian Series 60; it was not exclusive to the 6600. Receiving and installing malicious software mattered, rather than Bluetooth contact alone.

The original launch battery was the 850 mAh BL-5C; later batteries carrying the same name could have a different capacity.
