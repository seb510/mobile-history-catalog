The Xperia X10 was Sony Ericsson's first Android phone. Announced in 2009, it was already reported as entering shipment at the end of the first quarter of 2010. The catalog therefore uses 2010, the market-release period, rather than the year of its first reveal.

Sony Ericsson sought to stand apart through more than a large touchscreen and camera. Timescape gathered interactions with contacts into one stream, while Mediascape provided a dedicated hub for media. These were ways to carry the company's identity into Android, where competitors could share the same underlying platform.

The transition did not mean the brand abandoned its previous operating system all at once. In the same report, Sony Ericsson mentioned the Symbian-based Vivaz alongside the Android X10. That context matters: the company was still trying more than one platform, while Xperia gradually became the direction for its new smartphones.

In the timeline, the X10 captures that search: familiar Sony Ericsson strengths — camera, music and a distinctive interface — gained a new software foundation. Its launch operating system should be distinguished from later updates; features of the final firmware should not be attributed to the first units sold.
