The N70 was introduced on 27 April 2005 alongside the first wave of Nokia Nseries. It was a conventional keypad candybar, not a clamshell: only the rear cover slid, revealing and automatically activating the 2-megapixel camera. A dedicated shutter key brought the shooting experience closer to that of a compact camera.

The front camera served a different purpose: video calls over 3G. Together with photography, a flash, music player and stereo FM radio, it reflected the period's idea of a “multimedia computer”—several everyday devices converging in one smartphone.

Nokia also considered what happened after capture. XpressTransfer copied new photos and videos to a compatible PC, while XpressPrint let users print from the gallery over a cable, Bluetooth or a memory card. Before cloud photo libraries became common, these local connections between phone, computer and printer were an important part of using a camera phone.

The N70 brought these capabilities to the Nseries in a more compact, conventional form. This does not establish a particular sales ranking, but it shows how photography, video calling and installable applications gradually became familiar features of keypad smartphones.
