The W800i began Walkman's mobile story in 2005. Ericsson's Contact magazine described it as the first phone under the brand, introduced alongside other new products on 1 March. Sony Ericsson brought a familiar name in portable music to an everyday communication device.

- **Your own library.** Music began with personal files rather than streaming. A 512 MB card was included in the planned package, and MP3/AAC files transferred from a computer by cable. Manufacturer publications expressed capacity as an approximate song count; actual numbers depended on file size and encoding.
- **A player in your phone.** Accessories and straightforward track transfer mattered when competing with a separate music player for pocket space. Walkman communicated its purpose before the user opened a menu, while photography and calls remained part of the same device.

Alongside the K750i, it shows two Sony Ericsson priorities: photography as an everyday tool and music as a central habit. The ROKR E1 offers another contemporary comparison — file transfer and a music brand versus integration with a particular iTunes library.
