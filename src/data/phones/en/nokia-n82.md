Nokia unveiled the N82 on 14 November 2007 in Espoo under the slogan "It's what cameras have become" — a direct continuation of the N95's "It's what computers have become", now focused specifically on photography. The device returned to a candybar shape, dropping the slider that defined the N95 while keeping its GPS, Wi-Fi and Carl Zeiss optics.

The main difference from its predecessor was a xenon flash in place of an LED one. It produced a brighter flash closer to a compact camera's, better suited to low light, though it needed time to recharge its capacitor between shots. The 5-megapixel autofocus camera was protected by a lens cover that slid open together with the camera launch.

A-GPS and Nokia Maps carried over the N95's role as a pocket navigation tool, while TV-out and microSDHC support up to 32 GB extended the phone as a media hub. Nokia's starting estimate was about €450 before taxes and subsidies — the same segment as the N95 a year earlier.

Specifications have been checked against the official announcement and Wikipedia; the text does not repeat unverified claims about the N82 being the absolute first Nokia phone with a xenon flash.
