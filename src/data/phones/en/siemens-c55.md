The Siemens C55 shows how personalisation became important in the early 2000s even without a colour display or camera. Replaceable covers could change its appearance, while sound features made the phone recognisable before its owner even took it out of a pocket.

One distinctive feature was Sound Manager. According to Siemens' manual, users could record sounds, melodies and tones through the microphone, save them and assign them to different kinds of calls. Unlike phones limited to a few built-in alerts, the source of a ringtone could be a sound from the user's surroundings.

Games & More opened the way to downloadable Java applications, while WAP provided access to small mobile pages. Storage was very limited: the manual describes shared memory for sounds, graphics, applications and the SMS archive, and displays the remaining space in kilobytes. Modern ideas of full web browsing and large storage do not fit this experience.

In the timeline, the C55 marks a move from a phone just for calls to a personal device with custom sounds and programs. It pairs naturally with the Siemens M50: both belong to a moment when new capabilities arrived on a monochrome screen.
