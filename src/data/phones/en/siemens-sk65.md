The SK65 addressed a familiar early-smartphone problem: fitting a full QWERTY keyboard into a handset that looked like an ordinary candybar. Siemens solved it with a rotating body.

- **Two ways to type.** Turning the upper body through 90 degrees exposed the 37-key x2type keyboard. Closed, the phone worked with a conventional numeric keypad. The mechanism rotates; it does not slide.
- **Corporate messaging.** The SK65 was among the early non-BlackBerry phones to adopt BlackBerry Built-In for push email and corporate data. Its business focus gave the unusual keyboard a specific purpose.

The niche was narrow, but the rotating keyboard remains a memorable Siemens Mobile design from before the division's sale to BenQ in 2005.

Total memory was 64 MB, with around 30 MB available for user data. The technical portrait lists the latter figure.
