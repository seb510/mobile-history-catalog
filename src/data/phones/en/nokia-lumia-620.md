Nokia unveiled the Lumia 620 on 5 December 2012 at LeWeb as its third and, at the time, most affordable own Windows Phone 8 handset — a compact alternative to the mid-range Lumia 820 and flagship Lumia 920. Initial sales began in early 2013 in Asia and Africa, followed by Europe and other regions; the approximate unlocked retail price was around $249.

The 620 was the first Lumia with a new "double-shot" polycarbonate shell: an outer removable panel in one colour sat over an inner layer of another, and a second interchangeable panel was included in the box. Combined with bright colours (from lime and cyan to classic black and white), this made the phone part of a line built around personalisation, not just specs.

Despite the budget price, it kept some of the capabilities of larger Lumias: a 3.8-inch IPS display with ClearBlack technology for better outdoor readability, NFC, GPS with GLONASS, and Nokia's own services (Maps, Drive, City Lens). 512 MB of RAM and 8 GB of built-in storage with microSD support up to 64 GB reflected the priorities of the budget segment — enough for everyday use, without claiming flagship performance.

Within the Windows Phone lineup, the Lumia 620 marked Nokia's strategy of growing its audience through price and colour rather than specs alone. It shipped with Windows Phone 8 and could later update to 8.1; the feature set at launch shouldn't be confused with capabilities added by later OS updates.
