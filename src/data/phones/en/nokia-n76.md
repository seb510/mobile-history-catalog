Nokia showed the N76 on 7 January 2007 at CES in Las Vegas, with sales starting that May at a launch estimate of around €390 before taxes and subsidies. The model extended the Nseries line into an unusual clamshell format, departing from the candybars and sliders the series was known for.

Contemporaries called its 13.7 mm body one of the thinnest in the Nseries lineup at the time — that comparison applies specifically to the Nseries, not to clamshells in general, and shouldn’t be carried over to later models like the Nokia N8. The outer cover had its own display for the time and incoming calls, plus dedicated music-player keys accessible without opening the phone.

The main 2.4-inch screen, a 2-megapixel camera with an LED flash, and a front CIF camera for video calls rounded out Symbian OS 9.2 with the Series 60 3rd Edition interface running on a Freescale ARM11 processor. 26 MB of free built-in memory was offset by a microSD slot; Bluetooth 2.0 with A2DP support let you listen to music over wireless headphones.

In the timeline, the N76 is an example of how Nokia carried the Nseries' multimedia ambitions into a compact clamshell keypad form factor, between bulkier sliders like the N95 and the series' following models, the N81 and N82.
