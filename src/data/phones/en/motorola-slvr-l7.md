The SLVR L7 brought the RAZR design language to a candybar: a thin body, metal details and a flat keypad without a clamshell hinge. It was another branch of the same idea — a phone distinguished by silhouette and materials.

- **The shape comes first.** Its fixed body translated the visual identity of the RAZR into a different everyday gesture: the keypad was always exposed, with no lid to open.
- **iTunes depended on the version.** Motorola and Cingular's joint announcement of 31 January 2006 described USB music transfer and a 100-song limit for the US iTunes version. That carrier launch is not a universal release date for all L7 handsets, and iTunes support did not apply to every regional version.

A VGA camera, video recording and expandable storage complemented music playback. The library began on a computer, from which songs were copied to the phone. [Compare it with the ROKR E1](/en/compare/?a=motorola-rokr-e1&b=motorola-slvr-l7) to see two different bodies from the era of phone-based iTunes.
