Samsung introduced the Galaxy S III on 3 May 2012. Alongside its 4.8-inch HD Super AMOLED display and Android 4.0, the company emphasized interaction: the rounded body and software features were meant to make the phone more comfortable in everyday use.

Smart Stay used the front camera to detect when someone was looking at the screen and keep it illuminated. Direct Call could move from a message to a call when the phone was raised to the ear. These features represented another direction in competition: manufacturers built their own interaction patterns on top of the shared Android platform.

On 6 September 2012, Samsung reported 20 million units sold in 100 days; a later retrospective cited more than 40 million in seven months. Both figures describe specific periods, not a final lifetime total. [Compare the S III with the S4](/en/compare/?a=samsung-galaxy-s3&b=samsung-galaxy-s4), where the software emphasis became even more pronounced.
