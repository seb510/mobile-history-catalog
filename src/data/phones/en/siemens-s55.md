The 2002 S55 captures an early stage of mobile photography, when the camera could still be a separate module. QuickPic IQP-500 attached to the phone; the manual explained how to view, save and send pictures. This differed from the next generation, with cameras permanently built into the body.

- **A camera you could attach.** Bundles varied: compatibility with QuickPic does not mean every S55 included one in its box. Smaller images suited messages, while VGA offered a larger size. Display, memory and data-transfer limits shaped the photographic experience as much as the camera itself.
- **Before the cloud.** Bluetooth and IrDA supported data exchange and compatible accessories. The manual specified that they could not be active simultaneously: switching one on disabled the other.

Java applications and an organiser rounded out its business features. Compared with the S65, it shows how cameras moved inside the body and removable memory cards became important to multimedia. The S55 preserves a moment when a new capability could literally be attached to your phone.
