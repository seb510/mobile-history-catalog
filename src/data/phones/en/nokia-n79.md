Nokia announced the N79 on 25 August 2008, with retail availability following in October at a launch estimate of €350 before tax and carrier subsidy. The N79 replaced the earlier N78 in the Nseries line-up and was positioned as a sleeker, more compact alternative to the flagship N85 of the same generation.

The candybar body sold in three base colours — Canvas White, Seal Grey and Petrol Black — each with its own set of swappable rear covers (shades such as Sea Blue, Espresso Brown or Coral Red) that let owners change the phone's look without replacing the device. It's the same idea as the classic Xpress-on covers, just applied to a Symbian smartphone. The 5-megapixel camera with Carl Zeiss Tessar optics and a dual LED flash was a clear step up from the N78's 3.2-megapixel camera.

The hardware platform matched the senior Nseries models of the period: Wi-Fi, UMTS/HSDPA, built-in GPS with A-GPS support, an FM radio with a transmitter function, and microSDHC support up to 32 GB. Symbian OS 9.3 on S60 3rd Edition Feature Pack 2 gave access to the same application catalogue as the N95 or N82, without any of these components being a first for the platform.

Later, in January 2009, Nokia introduced a separate N79 Active variant with a sportier design and a different accessory set; that's a distinct model and shouldn't be confused with the base 2008 N79 described here.
