The Nokia 6210 arrived in 2000 as a business-oriented phone. Its significance was not limited to calendars and contacts: its WAP browser could use HSCSD, offering faster data transfer than a regular circuit-switched GSM connection.

Nokia listed HSCSD speeds of up to 43.2 kbps. Infrared allowed data exchange with compatible computers and printers, and the phone could also act as a modem. Mobile internet still depended on slow connections and time-based charges, but it was becoming a practical tool away from a desktop computer.

Alongside the mass-market Nokia 3310, the 6210 represents another side of 2000: business communication and mobile data were advancing alongside straightforward phones for calls and SMS.
