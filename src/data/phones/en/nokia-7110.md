In February 1999, Nokia introduced the 7110 as its first WAP-based “media phone.” The idea was not ordinary web browsing, but mobile access to news, weather, stock prices and other text-based services. Nokia said volume availability was planned for the second quarter of that year.

The phone used a NaviRoller to scroll through menus and confirm a selection with a press. Predictive text made composing messages on a numeric keypad faster. Together, these features showed how a button phone was adapting to new information services.

A spring-loaded cover protected the keypad and could be released by pressing a catch on the back; opening and closing it also answered and ended calls. This was not the Nokia 8110 featured in *The Matrix*: the similar sliding-cover gesture has often led to confusion between the models.
