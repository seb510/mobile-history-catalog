On 27 August 2003, Nokia introduced the 1100 at events in Moscow and New Delhi. The company explicitly positioned it for people new to mobile service and planned shipments to begin in the fourth quarter. Its place in the story is about bringing calls and messages within reach of a wider audience, not competing for the largest color screen.

The design considered everyday conditions: Nokia highlighted non-slip sides and reduced risk of damage from dust and rain. That does not mean the phone was waterproof or carried a modern certified IP rating. The monochrome display allowed the font size to be changed; large keys and straightforward navigation lowered the barrier to getting started.

A flashlight, alarm, stopwatch and two games added practical small touches to communication. Messaging included predictive text and distribution lists, while Xpress-on covers let owners personalize even the basic model. This was not simply a phone with fewer features, but a different set of priorities: clarity, everyday usefulness and affordability.

Alongside the multimedia Nokia 6230, the 1100 shows another direction in the same company's development in 2003–2004. Mobile history is not made up of flagships alone: phones that made mobile service itself familiar mattered too.
