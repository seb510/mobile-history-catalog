The HTC Dream, also known as the T-Mobile G1, was the first commercially available Android phone. Introduced on 23 September 2008, it went on sale in the US on 22 October. HTC, Google and T-Mobile entered a market increasingly shaped by services and applications as well as hardware.

- **Touch and physical input.** A touchscreen joined a sliding QWERTY keyboard and trackball. The design captures a transition: users touched the display but retained physical keys for longer messages. Gmail, YouTube and Google Maps with Street View brought familiar web services into the pocket.
- **A platform you could extend.** Android Market let users add applications and developers create new uses for the phone. The G1 began a platform adopted by multiple manufacturers, rather than simply offering another touchscreen rival to the iPhone.

The announced US price of $179 required a two-year voice-and-data agreement. It was not a universal retail price for the HTC Dream, and that carrier model matters when comparing devices across eras.
