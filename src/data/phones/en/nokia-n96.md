Nokia unveiled the N96 on 11 February 2008 at Mobile World Congress in Barcelona as a follow-up to the N95 — a "multimedia computer" focused primarily on video and television. Shipments began over summer and autumn that year (Europe in late August, North America in September); the exact date in any given country depended on the carrier and region.

The body kept the N95's dual-slider idea — a numeric keypad on one side, multimedia playback keys on the other — but added something the N95 lacked: a fold-out kickstand on the back. It let the phone stand on a table at a comfortable angle for watching video or a TV broadcast without holding it.

N96's main focus was media: 16 GB of built-in storage (generous for 2008) plus a microSD slot, a 5-megapixel camera with Carl Zeiss optics and dual LED flash, and support for DVB-H — a mobile-TV standard already being trialled in several European countries at launch. Receiving DVB-H broadcasts depended on a matching broadcast network existing in a given city; the phone supported the standard technically, which didn't guarantee service availability everywhere.

DVB-H ultimately never became a mass mobile-TV standard, and this part of the N96's design is best read as a bet on a technology that didn't catch on, not as a "signature" feature later lost. The rest of the phone is a logical continuation of the N95 line: the same dual slider, Symbian S60 3rd Edition and GPS, now with more storage and a kickstand for video.
