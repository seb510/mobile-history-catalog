Nokia began in Finland in 1865 with paper manufacturing. Through cables, rubber and electronics, the company moved into telecommunications and focused on mobile communications in the 1990s.

On the [3310](/en/phones/nokia-3310/) in 2000, the same number pad served for texting and gaming. With T9, composing a word took one press per letter. In Snake II, those keys steered the snake.

The hinge on the [N93](/en/phones/nokia-n93/) in 2006 changed how you held a phone to film. You could flip the screen open, turn it towards you and use the lower half as a camcorder grip. A year later, the [N95](/en/phones/nokia-n95/) used a two-way slider. Sliding the screen one way revealed the number pad, while sliding it the other way uncovered the music controls. Built-in GPS could place you on a map.

Microsoft acquired Nokia’s phone business in 2014. Phones bearing the Nokia name later returned through HMD Global.
