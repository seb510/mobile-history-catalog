Siemens is a German industrial company founded in 1847, long before mobile communications. Its phone division grew out of its telecommunications business. Models such as the C55, S55 and SL55 made the brand particularly recognisable during the early-2000s keypad era.

In 2001, the [SL45i](/en/phones/siemens-sl45i/) let listeners carry their music on a removable MultiMediaCard. They could load MP3 tracks onto the card and play them on the phone.

The [S55](/en/phones/siemens-s55/) took photos with a separate accessory in 2002. You plugged a QuickPic camera into the handset, took a picture, then used the phone to view it, save it or send it in a message.

In 2004, the [SK65](/en/phones/siemens-sk65/) offered a number pad for calls and a hidden keyboard for correspondence. A quarter turn of the body revealed QWERTY keys on either side of the screen.

In 2005, the mobile business was sold to Taiwan’s BenQ and continued under the BenQ-Siemens name. In this archive, Siemens stands for distinctive keypads, light signals, interchangeable covers and experiments with sound: features that made a phone recognisable even without its logo.
